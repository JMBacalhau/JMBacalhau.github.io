Multi-Objective Wide Corridor Routing: Enhancing Spatial Optimization for Infrastructure Planning

Abstract

Infrastructure planning, such as routing highways, powerlines, and railways, requires balancing complex spatial constraints, financial costs, and environmental impacts. Traditional Least-Cost Path (LCP) analyses often simplify these routes to one-dimensional lines and optimize for a single aggregated objective. Building upon recent advancements in spatial routing algorithms, this paper presents a novel methodological improvement: the Multi-Objective Wide A (MOA) algorithm. This approach not only accounts for the physical footprint of the infrastructure by optimizing wide corridors rather than single-pixel lines, but it also simultaneously evaluates competing objectives (e.g., financial vs. environmental costs) to generate a Pareto front of optimal solutions. Our results demonstrate that this method successfully identifies a diverse set of feasible, non-dominated wide corridors, providing decision-makers with a robust framework for complex spatial planning.
